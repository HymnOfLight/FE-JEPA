# PRE-REGISTRATION W9 -- unlabelled pool size (C1) and a mesh-independent scale (S)

**Branch:** wp9-pool. **Status:** r3 DRAFT (2 October 2026), not stamped; the six CONFIG_SHA256 lines below are open. Stamping fills them all at once, before session 1 (Sec. 8); post-stamp criterion changes are prohibited. **Authority:** this English file governs. **Publication:** every outcome below is publishable.

## 1. Questions
- **Q1 (C1).** At a fixed budget of 204,800 label-free AR steps per seed, does training on a larger unlabelled pool lower the relative energy gap in band (and, secondarily, in the tail and on held-out families)? Every deciding run so far trained on 1,024 instances. The theory note observes that, because the AR objective needs no reference solution, a surrogate can be trained on arbitrarily many unlabelled instances at assembly cost -- a statement about cost, with generalisation explicitly outside its scope; whether a larger pool lowers the error at a fixed step budget has not been measured, and neither verdict below bears on the note's statement. A larger pool is a larger sample of the same training family; H1 concerns that family's in-band distribution.
- **Q2 (S).** Does decoding with a scale that does not change with the mesh, and feeding the loads in a form that does not change with the mesh, reduce the GROWTH of the displacement error from the training mesh sizes (h 0.05-0.12) to a finer mesh (h 0.025)? This is KP4's failure mode; in 3D, wp8's post-hoc reading 4e found a fine-mesh amplitude deficit in it (point-token transformer: median c* 1.23 on the fine mesh against 1.00 in band; the label-free c_b on fixed geometries 0.98-1.01 at the training mesh sizes and 1.27 on the fine mesh). Asked only if session 1's rule 1 admits it (Sec. 4).

Exactly one of the two largest-pool configurations runs; "N_max" below is the one that ran. The factor 1/64 only sets the level of S's decoded output: on the first 256 instances of E1's training corpus (`scripts/w9_scale_factor.py`, assembly only; `records/wp9/scale_factor.json`), max|F| / sum|F| (component-wise) has median 0.0151 (10th-90th percentile 0.0096-0.0219, i.e. 0.61-1.40 times 1/64) and grows with the mesh size (rank correlation 0.67), so at training mesh sizes S asks the network for outputs of about E1's level on the median instance. Meshed again at R's mesh sizes, 16 of these geometries show at h = 0.025 a max|F| of 0.213 times its h = 0.12 value (median; range 0.204-0.225; h itself 0.208 times) and a sum|F| unchanged to within 0.3%: the scale of E1's arms shrinks with the element size, S's does not. S changes the decode scale and the load inputs together, and H2 tests them together; a SUPPORTED H2 is a statement about S as a whole, and which part carries the effect is not tested (the mechanism readings of Sec. 7 show whether the amplitude moved). The configurations are generated (`scripts/make_w9_configs.py`, from `configs/e1_2d_base.json`, which it refuses unless its canonical SHA-256 is PREREG_E1's stamped `4dfdea42...`); a test regenerates them byte for byte and checks that each differs from E1's base only in the keys named here (`evaluation`, `out`, `prereg_file`, `_comment`, `e8.pool_sizes`, `e8.ar_epochs`, `e8.reuse_from`, `e8.seed_offset`, and S's three model keys).

The 1,024 arm reuses E1's states because the default training path is bitwise unchanged against `wp8-lejepa` (Sec. 9); the N_max and 4,096 arms train with the same code, corpus, seeds and schedule length. S is compared with a freshly trained baseline on fresh seeds, not with E1's states: rule 1 admits S by reading E1's states on F5, and comparing S with the very states that were selected for a large fine-mesh error would bias H2 toward support. The fresh baseline runs whether or not S does: it is a coarse on-box check of the training path behind the reuse (other seeds, and the guard's 10% floor: only a gross difference shows; Sec. 7, 9). Activation checkpointing (rule 3) recomputes the same operations (bitwise identical on CPU, tested); on CUDA any two runs differ at round-off (atomic reductions), with or without it; E1's states were trained with it on.

A CPU pilot of S ran before the stamp (`scripts/w9_pilot_s.py`, `records/wp9/pilot_s_cpu/`; its questions and red flags were written into the script before it ran, and the script and its results were first committed together): toy scale (dim 32, depth 2, 3,072 steps per seed, two seeds per arm) on its own draws (corpus seed 920, an F5-like set seed 92005, an R-like set seed 92006; none of Sec. 3's sets). Its pre-declared red flags (a non-finite reading of S; S's in-band energy gap above twice the baseline's) did not fire; at that scale the baseline's median c* on the F5-like set was 2.4-2.7 and S's 0.94-0.98. It is exploratory: the scale does not carry over, and nothing here depends on it.

## 3. Evaluation sets
- **In band:** E1's validation split (256 instances) and the in-band holdout IB: 2,048 training-family instances drawn afresh by the training sampler (`fejepa.fe.ood2d.generate_inband`), labelled and pinned like the families below. H1 reads the two together (Sec. 5); H2, rule 1 and every F5 / in-band ratio read the validation split alone.
- **OOD-2D v1** (`src/fejepa/fe/ood2d.py`; evaluation only -- no training and no hyper-parameter reads them; rule 1 reads E1's states' errors on F5, which is why H2's reference is the fresh baseline): five families of 256 labelled instances, each changing one attribute of the training family and drawing the others from the training ranges: F1 4-6 holes (training 0-3); F2 slender plates, width 3.0-4.5 and height 0.6-0.8 (1.5-3.0, 0.8-1.5); F3 1-3 holes, all large (0.16-0.22) or all small (0.03-0.06) x min(w, h), one class per instance with probability 1/2 (0.06-0.16); F4 Poisson ratio 0.40-0.45 (0.25-0.38); F5 mesh size 0.025 (0.05-0.12). Secondary differences that come with the changed attribute: F1's radii are slightly smaller on average (0.106 against 0.109 x min(w, h)); F3 has no hole-free plates and its large holes may leave an edge ligament of 0.02 x min(w, h) (training at least 0.04).
- **R** (remesh set, evaluation only): 16 training-family geometries, each meshed at h = 0.12, 0.085, 0.05, 0.035, 0.025 (80 instances; only the mesh changes).
- The sets are defined by the committed generator and seeds (91001-91006 for F1-F5 and R, 91007 for IB) and generated once on the box in session 1 (`scripts/w9_make_ood2d.py`), labelled there by direct solve (the labels' cost is in each manifest's ledger; the runs buy nothing). Each manifest pins every file by SHA-256; every run verifies its holdouts before training (family name, files, labels) and records each manifest's SHA-256; the session-2 plan checks the manifests on disk against session 1's record, and the record's sizes and seeds against this section, before any arm runs, and plans no session 2 if any set failed to generate (in the sandbox, with gmsh 4.15.2, the committed generator drew all seven sets from these seeds without a failure: `records/wp9/ood2d_sandbox.json`); the adjudication requires the record to hold these sizes and seeds and every arm to report session 1's manifests.

## 4. Session 1 and the selection rules
Session 1 (`RUNBOOK_W9.md` Sec. 1) trains nothing. Its readings that select what session 2 runs are read by `scripts/w9_session1_decisions.py`, committed on 1 October 2026 (`1b32a34`, Stage 0a) before any reading existed and unchanged since; each rule is evaluated on its own, so a missing input leaves only the rules that read it undecided:
1. **S enters** iff E1's base states show, on F5 against the validation split, a displacement-error ratio (seed means) >= 1.5 AND a median c* on F5 >= 1.10. Then `w9_s_n1024` runs (the fresh baseline `w9_b_n1024` runs in every session 2).
2. **N_max and the schedule:** 25,600 with three concurrent workers if their host and device memory fit within 80% of the limits (memory audit C0.6), else 25,600 one seed at a time, else 12,800 with three workers, else one at a time; else no session 2 (the pool design is revisited).
3. **Activation checkpointing off** iff the AR step is >= 1.3x faster without it in E1's worker set-up AND rule 2's choice still fits without it. Undecided (no timing): on. The plan keeps it on for any other arm that would not fit without it (the same memory test).
4. and 5. Predictions only (training/validation gap ratio; the CG energy-drop score's rank correlation, for C2 later): no effect on any arm or criterion.

`scripts/w9_session2_plan.py` turns the decisions into session 2's command list and refuses to plan (STOP) unless: the required session-1 steps exited 0; the decision file is the frozen rules' output (their SHA-256) on the session's own readings (theirs), recomputed and identical, written on `prereg-w9`, which the checkout must also be; rules 1-2 are decided and a pool was found; the evaluation sets are complete, of Sec. 3's sizes and seeds, and the manifests on disk are the record's; E1's validation arrays were reproduced (Sec. 9); a GPU and E1's torch version are present; every selected configuration passes its dry run against this file. Workers and checkpointing are run-time settings (`run-config --workers`, `--activation-checkpointing`), recorded in each report; they change scheduling and memory, not the operations a unit computes (bitwise identical on CPU with single-threaded torch; on CUDA any two runs differ at round-off).

## 5. Measurements
For every arm, seed and evaluation set: the frozen metric suite per instance (relative energy gap, displacement relative L2, von Mises relative L2, peak von Mises error, critical-region recall), and the amplitude readings per instance (c* per load case, the battery form c_b, displacement error and energy gap after scaling each load case by its c*, energy norms of prediction and exact solution). Per-seed values are means over the set's instances; H1's in-band value of a seed is its mean over the validation split's and IB's 2,304 instances together (each set's mean weighted by its number of instances); arm values are seed means; tails are per-seed 90th percentiles of the per-instance energy gap (linear interpolation), seed-averaged; the F5 / in-band ratio (in band here: the validation split, Sec. 3) is formed per seed from the seed's mean displacement errors, and H2 reads the mean of these per-seed ratios; rule 1 (Sec. 4) reads the ratio of the seed means instead. Both are reported for every arm.

## 6. Hypotheses and verdicts
Noise guard (the E-series rule, PREREG_E1 r13): for arm values A (new) and B (reference) from three seeds each, rel = mean(A)/mean(B) - 1 and SE_rel = sqrt(s_A^2/3 + s_B^2/3) / mean(B) with sample standard deviations (n - 1); tau = max(10%, 2 x SE_rel). "Lower beyond the guard" means rel < -tau; "worse beyond the guard" means rel > tau. The tau applied is reported with each verdict, as are the per-seed values.
- **H1 (C1; primary).** Reference: the 1,024 arm. **H1 is SUPPORTED** iff the N_max arm's in-band relative energy gap (the validation split and IB together, Sec. 5) is lower beyond the guard; otherwise **NOT SUPPORTED** (reported as "worse" if it is worse beyond the guard). A non-finite per-seed value of the N_max arm is a divergence: NOT SUPPORTED (diverged); a non-finite value in the reference voids the comparison (H1 not evaluated). Properties of the rule. The reference is fixed (E1's states). On the validation split its energy gaps read 0.0374 / 0.0405 / 0.0371 by seed, mean 0.0383, and that spread comes from one instance: validation instance 233 has a gap of 0.760 in seed 1 against 0.011 in seeds 0 and 2; without it the seeds read 0.0375 / 0.0376 / 0.0372 (coefficient of variation 0.6%; 2.1% in E1's shaped arm, which has no such instance), and the reference mean is 2.3% above that level. Such a blow-up (one in E1's 7 x 256 instance-seed values) moves an arm's mean over 256 instances by about 2.6%. On the validation split alone, with tau at its 10% floor, H1 would have needed a reduction of about 7.9%, 10.5% or 13% of the reference's level without instance 233 as none, one or two of N_max's instance-seed values blew up; IB is added so that one instance no longer moves the threshold materially: on the 2,304 instances together a blow-up moves an arm mean by about 0.2-0.3% (instance 233 lifts the reference by about 0.3%). By simulation (`scripts/w9_sims.py`, `records/wp9/simulations.json`: E1's own validation arrays as the reference's validation part; per instance-seed jitter 8%, seed noise 0.5-2%, blow-ups at 0, 1 or 2 per 1,792 instance-seed values in every other part; 40,000 trials per case) no support occurred without a true effect, and a true reduction of 7.5% / 9% / 10% / 11% / 12.5% was supported in 0-3% / 1-24% / 46-80% / 76-100% / 96-100% of trials (on the validation split alone: 15-44% / 49-100% / 66-100% / 79-100% / 90-100%). Under r2's model instead (log-normal seed noise, coefficient of variation 4.9% in both arms), false support is 0.4%, and true reductions of 10% / 15% / 20% are supported in 48% / 91% / 99.8% of trials; at 10% noise, 4% and 29% / 52% / 75%. Near the threshold a verdict can still turn on a few instances; the per-seed medians and the intervals of Sec. 7 are reported beside it.
- **H2 (S; primary if S runs).** Reference: the fresh baseline `w9_b_n1024` (same seeds 3-5, pool, schedule and code; only S's three keys differ). **H2 is SUPPORTED** iff all of: (i) S's F5 displacement error is lower beyond the guard; (ii) S's F5 / in-band displacement-error ratio (the validation split's, Sec. 5) is lower beyond the guard (the growth itself shrinks, not only both levels); (iii) K1, the E-series parity rule on both in-band metrics, read on the validation split: neither S's energy gap nor its displacement error there is worse beyond the guard. Otherwise NOT SUPPORTED, with the failing conditions named. A non-finite per-seed value of S fails the condition it enters (reported as diverged); a non-finite value of the fresh baseline voids the comparison (H2 not evaluated). H2 reads the same 256 F5 instances and validation split that rule 1 read. The fresh seeds of both arms remove the selection on E1's training noise; the selection on the instances themselves remains (rule 1 admits S when these instances show a deficit under E1's states, and S's gain is largest where the deficit is), so it favours support, but in simulation it moves rel by less than one percentage point even when admission is a coin flip, against a tau of at least 10%. Properties by simulation (`scripts/w9_sims.py`; 200,000 trials, three seeds per arm, log-normal, in-band unchanged): at an F5 seed coefficient of variation of 10% / 15% / 20%, a support without a true effect occurs in about 3% / 4% / 5% of trials; a true F5 reduction of 20% is supported in about 74% / 47% / 32%, of 30% in 98% / 79% / 59%, of 50% in 100% / 100% / 97%. When S also lowers the in-band error, (ii) needs more (in-band -10%, F5 -30%: 61% at 15%). For scale: in wp8's 3D readings the oracle amplitude c* cut the transformer's fine-mesh displacement error by 68% but its fine / in-band ratio only from 8.6 to 5.9, because it lowered the in-band error too.
- Two questions, two verdicts; no multiplicity correction (they are separate claims, each reported with its own threshold). A primary arm without a report, or with a report the adjudication refuses (Sec. 8), leaves its own hypothesis NOT EVALUATED (recorded); the other is still adjudicated.
- **What a verdict licenses.** SUPPORTED: on these seeds and instances, at this step budget, model and training family, the new arm is lower than the reference beyond tau (for H2, each of its conditions holds). NOT SUPPORTED: no such reduction was shown; it is not evidence that the effect is absent or small. A reduction smaller than tau cannot be shown by design, and one near tau is missed about half the time (H1: a true reduction of 7.5% is supported in at most 3% of trials, one of 10% in about half to four fifths; H2 at an F5 seed spread of 15%: a 20% reduction is supported in about half the trials). No verdict is restated as "no effect"; each is reported with rel, SE_rel, tau and the per-seed values.

## 7. Secondary readings (reported, no verdict; exploratory)
- C1: the in-band tail; the validation split and IB each on its own; every OOD family's energy gap and displacement error; the c*-scaled readings; the curve 1,024 -> 4,096 -> N_max for every metric; each comparison with the Sec. 6 guard computed and stated (about a hundred comparisons: exploratory, not tests; without a true difference, at seed noise of 10-20% a comparison is lower beyond the guard in about 4-6% of cases and worse beyond it about as often).
- C1 against session 1's prediction (rule 4): the predicted reading beside the observed in-band change.
- The fresh baseline against E1's states (in band as H1 reads it, and on every set; different seeds): a coarse check of the reuse and of the training path on the box (only a gross difference crosses the guard); and N_max against the fresh baseline, beside H1. A difference between the fresh baseline and E1's states beyond the guard on the in-band energy gap, or a diverged fresh baseline, is reported with H1 as a flag; it does not change H1's verdict.
- S (if it runs): its readings on F1-F4 against the fresh baseline (does S cost anything elsewhere); mechanism -- the median c* on F5 (near 1 if S removes the amplitude deficit) and, on R, c_b and the predicted energy norm per mesh size relative to each geometry's coarsest mesh, beside the exact solution's own ratio (S's should follow the exact one; the baseline's is expected to fall as h shrinks); the F5 / in-band ratios of the 1,024 arm, the fresh baseline and S; a growth reading on R that rule 1 never read: per seed, R's mean displacement error at h = 0.025 over its mean at h = 0.12, 0.085 and 0.05 (16 geometries, only the mesh changes), S against the fresh baseline with the guard. If the fresh baseline does not show the deficit rule 1 admitted S on (its F5 / in-band ratio of seed means, rule 1's form, below 1.5, or its median c* on F5 below 1.10), a NOT SUPPORTED H2 is reported as uninformative about S's mechanism; the verdict stands.
- Beside H1 and each H2 condition (no criterion): the same guard on per-seed medians over the instances (for the ratio, the per-seed ratio of the medians); rel with a Welch 95% interval; a 95% interval for rel from resampling the instances (2,000 resamples, the same instances for both arms and every seed, the seeds kept). The seed guard ignores instance sampling; these readings show how much of a verdict rests on a few instances.
- Everything per seed as well as seed-averaged.

## 8. Execution mechanics (exact)
- Stamping, before session 1: `fejepa prereg configs/<arm>.json --stamp --prereg-file PREREG_W9.md` for all six configurations (each fills the line named after it; the guard refuses every arm while any line is open); this file's SHA-256 (last line), computed over the file with that line reading `PREREG_W9_SHA256 = <record after commit>` (a file cannot contain its own hash); commit; tag `prereg-w9`; push; the remote tag is verified before session 1. `scripts/stamp_prereg_w9.py` does the filling and the hash, refuses anything but this draft, and verifies the result. The branch is frozen until session 2 has returned: the box checks that `git describe --tags --match prereg-w9` prints exactly `prereg-w9`. Session 1 and session 2 then run on that commit, in one visit or two.
- Session 2 (`RUNBOOK_W9.md` Sec. 2): the commands `scripts/w9_session2_plan.py` writes, in this order: the 1,024 arm (evaluation only, minutes), N_max, the fresh baseline, S (if admitted), 4,096 -- the primary comparisons first. Each is a `run-config` of a stamped configuration with the run-time settings of rules 2 and 3; a restart uses `--reuse-states` (D9; recorded in the report). The command script checks before every arm that the code is still `prereg-w9`, keeps every attempt's log and time-stamps each attempt; it skips an arm whose report is complete and restarts one whose states exist without a report.
- Economy: label policy `asis`; every run's solve ledger reads 0 (the validation labels exist; the holdouts' labels were bought at generation and are counted in their manifests).
- Adjudication: `scripts/adjudicate_w9.py`, from the two returns. It recomputes session 1's decisions with the frozen rules from the returned readings. It refuses to adjudicate at all when the session is not intact: a decision file that differs from the recomputation, was written by other rules, from other readings, or on another commit than `prereg-w9`; an incomplete OOD-2D record, one whose sets are not Sec. 3's sizes and seeds, or an R manifest that is not the record's; a plan made from another decision file; a session-2 return without its plan, status file or provenance file; a report the provenance file lists that is missing or has another SHA-256; an arm the status file shows finished without its report. It refuses a single report -- leaves it out and records why -- that is not a guard-verified run of this file under its own CONFIG_SHA256 line, ran on another commit, differs from E1's base beyond the keys of Sec. 2, is not the stamped arm for its role, ran on another corpus or seeds, does not hold one evaluation per seed with a value for each of the set's instances, or whose holdout manifests are not session 1's; a 1,024 arm that is not E1's states (SHA-256 against E1's records copy, reused through that same file) or does not reproduce E1's validation arrays (largest relative deviation <= 1e-4; a non-finite value never reproduces); an N_max or S arm the rules did not select. It records as deviations: a planned arm without a report, run-time settings other than the plan's, restarts and repeated attempts, resumed units, a non-zero solve ledger, and a software stack or GPU other than E1's (H1 compares with states trained on E1's stack). Secondary readings never block a verdict. The verdict file records every input's SHA-256 and the adjudicating code's; adjudicating code that differs from `prereg-w9`'s (the adjudication's own files, the stamped-line reader and the frozen rules), committed or not, is recorded as a deviation.

## 9. Preconditions
- Suite green on the stamped head.
- The default training path is bitwise identical to `wp8-lejepa` (`scripts/regress_against_branch.py`, CPU, both sides in their own processes, fresh and restart passes): on a miniature of E1's base configuration (2 states, 162 numbers) and of the Phase-2b configuration (6 states, 2,590 numbers) -- identical (`records/wp9/regression_wp8_*.json`; `records/wp9/README.md` names the code they ran). This supports the reuse of E1's states. E1's states were trained at `cdac731` (tag `prereg-e1`); between it and `5f8e2df` the source changed only in a post-hoc analysis module, an atomic JSON write and the saved-state container (`_metadata` kept: same tensors, other file bytes), none on the training path. With E1's torch version on the box and the reproduction of E1's validation arrays (inference), this is the basis for reusing E1's states; the training path is re-checked on the box coarsely, by the fresh baseline against E1's states (Sec. 7).
- On the box before session 2 (checked by `w9_session2_plan.py`): every required session-1 step exited 0; the five OOD families, R and IB generated and verified; E1's per-instance validation arrays reproduced by the C0 evaluation of E1's states (largest relative deviation <= 1e-4; inference only, so 0 or round-off is expected); rules 1-2 decided; a GPU and E1's torch version.
- Cost: about 3 h per trained three-seed arm with three concurrent workers (E1's base arm took 2 h 58 min for 3 x 204,800 steps on this box), plus minutes per worker of data preparation at 25,600 and minutes per state of evaluation on 3,664 instances; N_max one seed at a time, if rule 2 so decides, about 5-9 h (unmeasured); the 1,024 arm, 10-30 min. Session 2 in all: about 10 h without S, about 13 h with S; 2-6 h more if N_max runs one seed at a time. Session 1: about 1 h.
