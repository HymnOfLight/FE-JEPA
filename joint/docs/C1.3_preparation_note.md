# Stage C1.3: two exploratory checks before the pre-specification

10 October 2026. Both checks were made after gate G-C1a2 and before any pre-specification of stage C1.3. Neither is a gate, neither was pre-specified, and nothing here is evidence for or against C1.3's hypothesis.

## 1. How large must the reference model be?

**The constraint.** The transformer of the project attends over all nodes. The C plan of 9 October 2026 proposed a limit of 30,000 unknowns for the half model (gate G-C0, a threshold for the project owner to confirm), because full attention at about 37,000 nodes had been measured at 14.8 s a step.

**The higher-fidelity model** of G-C1a2 (`fejoint/joint_hf.py`) is built on a structured grid.
- The fine spacing needed in the welds (a quarter of the in-plane spacing or a fifth of the leg) and across the bearing rings runs through the whole model.
- Its coarse mesh has about 38,500 nodes.
- The coarsest settings tried (in-plane spacing 10 mm, one cell across the bearing ring, one layer of cells in the end plate, the beam flanges and the bolt head) still give 23,690 to 23,820 nodes.

**The idealised model** of G-C1a (`fejoint/joint.py`, the options of `checks/gc1a_run.py`) was solved on coarser meshes and compared with its own fine-mesh values (`probes/c13_scale.py`, `probes/c13_scale.json`):

| Mesh | Nodes | Unknowns | $S / S_{fine}$, FS1 to FS4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 mm, one layer | 4,040 to 4,170 | 11,000 to 11,400 | 1.007 to 1.012 |
| 10 mm, two layers | 5,230 to 5,380 | 14,500 to 14,900 | 1.011 to 1.016 |
| 7.5 mm, two layers | 7,330 to 7,400 | 20,300 to 20,500 | 0.992 to 1.005 |
| 5 mm, two layers (G-C1a's medium) | 12,310 to 12,450 | 34,400 to 34,800 | 0.997 to 1.007 |

**Distances to state.** On the fine meshes the higher-fidelity model is 1.9% to 3.3% stiffer than the idealised one (G-C1a2). That is with its welds as meshed, 0.90 (flanges) and 0.92 (web) of the fillets; full-size welds would widen the distance. Both models are about 2 to 3 times stiffer than the tests.

**Consequence for C1.3.** This is a recommendation for the project owner to decide.
- Train against the idealised model on the 10 mm or the 7.5 mm mesh.
- State three distances: about 1% to its own fine mesh, 2% to 3% to the higher-fidelity model, and 2 to 3 times to the tests.
- This departs from the consequence written in G-C1a2's pre-specification, which named the higher-fidelity model as the reference for C1.3. The reason is its size.

## 2. Does keeping the contact unknowns non-negative stall training?

**The question.** C1.3 trains a network on the total potential energy, without solutions. On the contact face the predicted normal displacement must not penetrate, or the energy can fall below the solution's and the objective no longer bounds the error (`docs/contact_energy_note.md`). The C plan asked whether the map that keeps those unknowns non-negative makes training stall, and planned a few GPU hours on a two-dimensional T-stub to find out. An earlier direct optimisation of single fields (`probes/param_probe.py`, no network) had found ReLU stuck, softplus good with a small learning rate, the square good, and no constraint penetrating.

**What was run** (`probes/c13_net/`, on the CPU here):
- **Problem** (`tstub2d.py`): half of one T-stub of a symmetric pair, plane strain, per unit width.
  - The plane between the flanges is a rigid, frictionless foundation for the underside of the flange.
  - The bolt is a spring from the top of the flange to that plane; the web is pulled by a uniform traction.
  - Quadrilaterals with incompatible modes; 159 nodes, the same topology for every geometry.
  - The exact solutions come from the active set solver of the joint model.
  - Its checks pass: rigid modes and constant strain on a distorted element, pure bending exact with one element through the thickness, the optimality conditions, and the energy identity $\Pi(v) - \Pi^* = \tfrac12\|v - u^*\|_K^2 + \lambda^{*T} v_c$.
- **Family**:
  - $t_f$ 8 to 20 mm, $m$ 20 to 45 mm, $n$ 15 to 40 mm, bolt spring 3,000 to 30,000 N/mm per mm, uniform or log-uniform;
  - 512 geometries for training and 128 others for evaluation;
  - the contact zone is the tip of the flange: 0 to 6 of the 27 nodes on the contact face, 1 in the median; 13% of the evaluation geometries have no contact at all.
- **Network** (`net_probe.py`, JAX):
  - a transformer of 3 layers, width 64 and 4 heads over the nodes, permutation equivariant;
  - node features: coordinates, boundary labels and the geometry;
  - two outputs per node, scaled by a displacement scale computed from the geometry without any solve;
  - on the contact face the vertical output passes through the map under test.
- **Training**:
  - the energy of each geometry over the matching energy scale, also computed without any solve;
  - Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$ (better than $3 \times 10^{-4}$ in a 1,000-step check on the unconstrained variant), warm-up 200 steps, cosine decay, batch 32;
  - 3,000 steps, one seed.
- **Control**: the same network with the square map, trained on the exact solutions of the 512 training geometries by mean squared error.
- A variant with softplus at $T = 10^{-2}$ was dropped to save time.

**Results** on the 128 evaluation geometries, after 3,000 steps:

| Map on the contact face | Energy gap, median (90th percentile) | Stiffness error $F/\Delta$, median (90th percentile) | Contact set exact | Contact nodes missed, mean share | Geometries that penetrate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| None | −16% (+2%) | −12% (−1%) | 13% | 0.01 | 86% |
| ReLU | 3.8% (9.1%) | +4.9% (+12.7%) | 66% | 0.28 | 0 |
| Softplus, $T = 10^{-3}$ | 3.2% (8.2%) | +3.7% (+11.4%) | 63% | 0.29 | 0 |
| Square | 10.7% (18.7%) | +15.4% (+27.2%) | 13% | 0.87 | 0 |
| Square, supervised control | 20.3% (109%) | +0.1% (+0.9%) | 13% | 0.80 | 0 |

- The energy gap is $(\Pi(u) - \Pi^*)/|\Pi^*|$. The stiffness error is the predicted $F/\Delta$ over the exact one, minus 1, with $\Delta$ the gap between the flanges at the web.
- The contact set counts as exact when the nodes predicted to lie within $10^{-3}$ times the displacement scale of the foundation are exactly the nodes in contact in the solution. A prediction with no contact anywhere scores 13%. The exact solution itself scores 96% by this measure: in 5 geometries, nodes next to the contact zone lift by less than the tolerance.

**Reading.**
1. **No stall from ReLU or softplus in a network.** Unlike the direct optimisation, ReLU did not get stuck, presumably because the parameters are shared: a node whose own gradient is cut off still moves with the others. Softplus had the smaller energy gap and stiffness error, ReLU the slightly better contact set; one seed cannot separate them.
2. **The square is slow to close contact.** Its derivative vanishes at zero, so nodes that should touch presumably approach the foundation ever more slowly. After 3,000 steps it had closed none of them in any geometry: the mean share of contact nodes missed was 0.867, the most possible when 13% of the geometries have no contact, against 0.28 to 0.29 for ReLU and softplus. Its energy gap was three times the others'.
3. **Without a constraint the network penetrates.** In 86% of the geometries the face sinks into the foundation. In the median geometry the energy falls 16% below the solution's and the T-stub comes out 12% too soft. This confirms, in a network, that non-penetration is a condition of the objective.
4. **The label-free fields err on the stiff side**, as a Ritz approximation does: the energy gap is small, presumably because the soft modes are not yet resolved. The supervised control reads the stiffness almost exactly at the one point that defines it. Its fields carry five to six times the energy error of the ReLU and softplus fields and about twice that of the label-free square, and it misses the contact too.

**Limits.** One seed; 3,000 steps on a CPU; a small network; two dimensions; contact zones of a few nodes; a fixed mesh topology. The supervised control used the square map, so its energy and contact results are confounded with the map.

**Consequences for C1.3** (recommendations):
- Map the contact unknowns with softplus at $T = 10^{-3}$ of the displacement scale or with ReLU, as equal candidates; not with the square.
- The separate GPU probe on the two-dimensional T-stub is no longer needed for this question. Before the pre-specification, the GPU is needed only for a short cost probe on the three-dimensional joint mesh.
- The pre-specification should run at least two seeds per arm, since one seed cannot show whether the differences between ReLU and softplus exceed the variation between seeds.

## 3. Second seed (added the same evening)

The three constrained maps were run again with seed 2, all else as in Section 2 (`probes/c13_net/run_seed2.sh`, `results_*_s2.json`).

| Map | Seed | Energy gap, median (90th percentile) | Stiffness error, median (90th percentile) | Contact set exact | Contact nodes missed, mean share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ReLU | 1 | 3.8% (9.1%) | +4.9% (+12.7%) | 66% | 0.28 |
| ReLU | 2 | 6.1% (17.2%) | +7.8% (+17.1%) | 58% | 0.27 |
| Softplus, $T = 10^{-3}$ | 1 | 3.2% (8.2%) | +3.7% (+11.4%) | 63% | 0.29 |
| Softplus, $T = 10^{-3}$ | 2 | 4.2% (9.4%) | +5.0% (+14.4%) | 66% | 0.28 |
| Square | 1 | 10.7% (18.7%) | +15.4% (+27.2%) | 13% | 0.867 |
| Square | 2 | 17.8% (32.5%) | +28.8% (+45.4%) | 13% | 0.867 |

**Reading.**
- In both seeds softplus had the smaller energy gap and stiffness error than ReLU. The contact set went one way in each seed.
- The spread between the two seeds of one map, 3.8% to 6.1% for ReLU, is as large as the difference between the maps. Two seeds rank softplus first on these measures, but they do not show that the difference exceeds the variation between seeds.
- The square closed no contact in either seed and had the largest errors in both.
- The recommendation stands: softplus or ReLU, not the square. C1.3's pre-specification should run at least three seeds per arm if it compares the maps.
