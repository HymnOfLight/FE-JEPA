# Stage C1.3: the joint family and its data (draft for the pre-specification)

10 October 2026. This note records code written before C1.3's pre-specification, so that the pre-specification can fix its choices. **No reference solution of any family member has been computed.** Only the tested details and one sampled member were solved, in tests that check the export against the joint model.

## Choices already made by the project owner

- **Reference model.** The surrogate is trained and judged against the idealised model of G-C1a (`fejoint/joint.py`): support "contact", bolts "head_contact", head tilt stiffness $4 E_b I_s / L_b$, bonded regulariser $10^{-6}$.
- **Mesh.** The 10 mm mesh with one layer of cells in the end plate and the beam flanges is the default. The 7.5 mm mesh with two layers is the alternative. On FS1 to FS4 they come within 0.7% to 1.2% and −0.8% to +0.5% of the fine mesh (`docs/C1.3_preparation_note.md`).
- **The pre-specification is written after 15 October 2026.**

## The family (`fejoint/family.py`; ranges are drafts)

Eight parameters are sampled uniformly and independently. Layouts that break a rule are rejected and redrawn, with about 85% accepted.

| Parameter | Meaning | Range, mm | FS1 to FS4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| tp | end plate thickness | 8 to 22 | 10.06 to 20.02 |
| eX | top edge to bolt row 1 | 25 to 40 | 29.74 to 30.10 |
| p | row 1 to row 2, across the tension flange | 80 to 100 | 89.95 to 90.14 |
| p23 | row 2 to row 3 | 190 to 220 | 204.84 to 205.90 |
| w | gauge | 80 to 110 | 89.88 to 89.93 |
| o | overhang of the plate beyond the flanges, each side | 0 to 15 | 0, after the snap |
| LX | top edge to the beam's top face | 55 to 85 | 68.90 to 69.86 |
| c_bot | plate below the beam's bottom face | 20 to 45 | 31.09 to 32.05 |

**Fixed**
- The beam, the column flange thickness, the bolts and the load arm, at FS1's values.
- $E = 210{,}000$ MPa and $\nu = 0.3$ for every part.
- The tested details enter as family members with their own plate and bolt layouts.

**Rules**
- Every bearing patch (radius 14.1 mm) clears the beam's flanges and web and the plate's edges by at least 2 mm.
- The rows are in order.
- The plate is at least as wide as the flanges.
- An overhang below 2 mm is set to zero. Otherwise a grid line on the plate's edge and one on the flange's edge would leave a sliver of cells through the whole model.

## The data (`fejoint/export.py`)

Each member is exported in nodal form: three unknowns per node, node-major, as in the repository's archives.

**The heads are condensed out exactly.** The nine head unknowns (a plane $a + b\,dy + c\,dz$ for each of three bolts) carry no constraint and no load. Eliminating them leaves the Schur complement $S = A_{xx} - A_{xh} A_{hh}^{-1} A_{hx}$, which has the same solution and the same minimum. The head values are recovered as $h = -A_{hh}^{-1} A_{hx} x$; the export stores that map.

**The constraints stay simple bounds** on two kinds of unknown:
- the $u_x$ of every node on the column face;
- the $x$ slot of every node on a bearing patch, which holds the node's separation from its head plane instead of $u_x$.

The network keeps exactly these unknowns non-negative.

**One export holds:**
- the nodes and cells;
- $S$ and the load;
- the Dirichlet mask ($u_z = 0$ on the symmetry plane) and the non-negative mask;
- six node flags: symmetry plane, column face, bearing patch, load section, DT1 section, end plate;
- the reading of the paper's rotation, from the DT1 section's mean deflection, with its constants;
- a displacement scale computed without any solve: the half beam's cantilever deflection under $P/2$, or with a segment the segment's deflection under its end shear and moment;
- the metadata.

**Size.** FS1 on the 10 mm mesh has 4,064 nodes, 12,192 unknowns and 683,000 nonzeros in $S$.

**Beam segment** (`beam_segment`, added the same evening).
- As in `fejoint.joint.analyse`, the beam can be meshed only up to a segment, with its end section rigid and carrying the statically equivalent shear and moment. The deflection at DT1 is completed by beam theory.
- The section's three unknowns carry load. Condensing them leaves a nodal load, the map that recovers them, and a constant in the energy, which moves no gradient and cancels in every energy gap.
- The end section's own $u_x$ and $u_y$ are then no unknowns; they are masked like Dirichlet unknowns.
- On FS1 to FS4 (10 mm mesh; `probes/c13_segment_effect.json`):

| Beam | Nodes | $S$ over the full beam's |
|---|---|---|
| Full, about 1 m | 4,040 to 4,170 | 1 |
| 300 mm segment | 2,160 to 2,240 | 1.002 to 1.009 |
| 400 mm segment | 2,360 to 2,440 | 1.003 to 1.010 |

**Checks** (`tests/test_family_export.py`):
- the sampler is deterministic, every sample is valid, the ranges hold, and an invalid layout is rejected;
- on FS1 and on a sampled member, the exported problem solved by the active set method reproduces `fejoint.joint.analyse` to $10^{-12}$ in the stiffness and the displacements;
- Clapeyron's identity $\Pi^* = -\tfrac12 f^T u^*$ holds;
- the masks do not overlap and only $x$ slots are constrained;
- the energy identity $\Pi(v) - \Pi^* = \tfrac12\|v - u^*\|_S^2 + \lambda^{*T} v_c$ holds for an arbitrary $v$;
- with a 300 mm segment, the export reproduces `analyse(beam_segment=300)` to $10^{-12}$, and Clapeyron's identity holds with the section's load (8 tests in all).

## What the repository needs (not written yet)

The repository's energy anchor already takes $(K, F, \text{Dirichlet mask})$ per instance, so $S$ plugs in as $K$. Three additions are needed:
1. **Features.** A joint branch for the node features: the six flags and the family parameters. The current geometry descriptor is for boxes with cavities.
2. **The decoder.** Apply the non-negative map to the masked unknowns before the anchor: softplus at $10^{-3}$ of the displacement scale, or ReLU, per `docs/C1.3_preparation_note.md`.
3. **Archives.** An archive for hexahedral meshes, or a tetrahedral split used only for the features.

## The cost probe (`probes/c13_cost/cost_probe.py`)

- One training step as the repository's runner takes it, on exported family members:
  - the repository's encoder, with its activation checkpointing, and a copy of its field decoder;
  - softplus on the non-negative unknowns;
  - the repository's energy anchor;
  - an Adam step.
- Phase-2b's model is the default: width 256, depth 8, 8 heads.
- It ran here with torch 2.12.1, the version on the GPU host, from the repository's wp9-pool branch, as a functional check on a small model (width 64, depth 2) on two CPU threads:
  - 2.1 s a step on the 10 mm mesh (3,930 to 4,150 nodes);
  - 3.9 s a step on the 7.5 mm mesh (7,390 to 7,440 nodes).
- The timing that matters is the GPU's, with the full model: a run of a few minutes on the GPU host (`--device cuda`). It waits for wp9 and CM2D.

## An end-to-end smoke test (`probes/c13_cost/smoke_train.py`)

**Set-up.** One family member (seed 999, to be kept out of any later sample), the 10 mm mesh, the small model, one instance, trained on the CPU by the energy alone. It was compared with that instance's exact solution.

| Model | Run | Steps | Energy gap at the end | Predicted over exact $S$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full beam, 4,100 nodes | Default start, one-cycle learning rate up to $10^{-3}$ | 300 | 1.30 | −0.83 |
| Full beam | Last layer zeroed, constant learning rate $10^{-3}$ | 400 | 0.99 (0.88 at step 375) | −0.96 |
| 300 mm segment, 2,190 nodes | Last layer zeroed, constant learning rate $10^{-3}$ | 400 | 0.36 (0.24 at step 350) | 1.77 |
| 300 mm segment | Last layer zeroed, one-cycle learning rate up to $10^{-3}$ | 3,000 | 0.036 | 1.065 |

With the segment and 3,000 steps the contact on the column face is also forming: 17.5% of its nodes predicted in contact against 12.1% in the solution, intersection over union 0.65.

**Reading.**
- **The pieces fit.** On the full beam the energy falls to a gap of about 1, from about 18,000 with the default start and from 22 with the zeroed one (after its first step). The non-negative unknowns stay non-negative.
- **The full beam learns slowly.** Within 400 steps the network captured at most about 12% of the energy. It had not formed the global deflection: the deflection at DT1 was near zero, so the reading was dominated by the subtracted beam rotation and came out negative. Presumably the 1 m cantilever's bending and the joint's rotation, the softest modes, are the slowest to learn.
- **The segment learns much faster.** With the 300 mm segment the gap fell to about 0.3 in the same 400 steps. After 3,000 steps (ten minutes on the CPU) it was 3.6%, with the stiffness 6.5% high, on the stiff side as for a Ritz approximation.
- **These are smoke tests, not results about C1.3.** Each is one instance, which the network only has to fit, not a family it must generalise over; a small model; a CPU. Each run is a single seed.

**For the pre-specification.**
- **Shorten the beam** to the 300 mm segment the C plan foresaw. It halves the nodes, changes $S$ by less than 1%, and learns much faster here.
- **Budget the GPU run** on a small pilot of label-free training over the family on the shortened model, before the stamp, as Phase-2b's pilot did.
