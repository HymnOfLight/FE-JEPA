# PRE-REGISTRATION FINE3D -- the three-dimensional fine-mesh failure: a mesh-independent scale on the token bottleneck (R2a) and label-free fine-tuning at the fine mesh (R3)

**Branch:** wp9-pool. **Status:** r5 DRAFT (16 October 2026), not stamped; the four CONFIG_SHA256 lines below are open. Stamping fills them all at once, before any run (Sec. 8); post-stamp criterion changes are prohibited. **Authority:** this English file governs. **Publication:** every outcome below is publishable; any write-up reports every verdict of Sec. 6 with its rel, tau and per-seed values, R3's fine displacement reading beside R3-E and R3-V (Sec. 6), R3's labelled arm beside the Phase-2b record of the same fine-tuning (Sec. 7), and the R2b recommendation as Sec. 6 computes it.

## 1. Questions
In three dimensions the label-free (AR) transformer of the Phase-2b run loses accuracy on the fine mesh it never trained on: on P3's fine evaluation set (lc 0.0374; about 36,000 nodes per instance, median; 15,175 to 71,749) its displacement error is 8.62 times its in-band error (seed means 0.258 and 0.0300; the manuscript's Sec. 7). wp8's post-hoc reading 4e found an amplitude deficit in it: the energy-optimal amplitude c* (label-free; its "median" here and below is the mean over seeds of each seed's median over the instances and load cases) is 1.23 on the fine set against 1.00 in band, and scaling each load case by its c* lowers the fine displacement error by 68%. wp9 (PREREG_W9, H2 SUPPORTED) cut the analogous growth in two dimensions with S, a decode scale and load features that do not change with the mesh: the F5 displacement error fell by 76.0%, and its ratio to the in-band error by 74.1% (4.67 to 1.21).
- **Q-R2a.** Does S reduce the growth of the displacement error from the training mesh sizes (lc 0.0579-0.0906) to the fine mesh in three dimensions? Asked of the token bottleneck at M = 1,024 tokens (PREREG_E2's arm; retired by E2's K1 as a Phase-3 candidate, but trained in 2.5 h per seed against the transformer's 27.3 h). Its amplitude deficit is larger than the transformer's: its median c* on the fine set is 1.74 / 2.06 / 1.50 by seed (1.00 in band), and c* lowers its fine error by 78%; its growth is smaller -- the fine error is 6.43 times the in-band one (seed means 0.574 and 0.0892) -- because its in-band error is three times the transformer's. R2a is a mechanism test: it decides, by the rule of Sec. 6, whether S is worth running on the transformer (R2b: the transformer with and without S on three fresh seeds each as simulated in Sec. 6, six runs of 27.3 h of training, with its own pre-registration).
- **Q-R3.** Starting from the Phase-2b AR states, does fine-tuning with the AR objective -- the energy alone, no reference solution -- on 16 fine instances lower the fine-mesh energy gap and von Mises error below those of the same states fine-tuned with the displacement labels of the same 16 instances, on the same schedule, in the same session? P3's labelled few-shot fine-tuning at budget 16 brought the displacement error down to 0.0531 (seed mean; 1.8 times the in-band 0.0300) but not the energy gap (0.0702, 7.7 times the in-band 0.0091) or the von Mises error (0.166, 3.0 times 0.0554). The manuscript (Sec. 7, 9) states that label-free adaptation at the fine mesh was not tested.

## 2. R2a: arms
| Configuration | Seeds | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `configs/r2a_b_m1024.json` | 3-5 | E2's M = 1,024 arm trained again on fresh seeds (`e8.seed_offset = 3`): S's reference. |
| `configs/r2a_s_m1024.json` | 3-5 | As `r2a_b_m1024` with S: `model.decode_scale = "l1"` with `model.decode_scale_factor = 2^-10` (the decoded field is multiplied by 2^-10 times the sum of the absolute values of the battery's nodal force components instead of by the largest of them) and `model.features.load_density = true` (per-node load densities -- each nodal force divided by the boundary area (traction) or the volume (body load) the node carries -- and a load summary of mesh-free ratios). |

Both: everything else is E2's configuration (`configs/e2_m1024.json`, CONFIG_SHA256 `526a5688...` in PREREG_E2): the corpus `runs/data3d_phase2` (manifest `dc75628290bb...`) and its fine set `runs/data3d_phase2_fine` (`0f859ad98a01...`), split 256 / seed 1, the 1,024-instance training prefix, 200 epochs at lr 1e-3 (204,800 AR steps per seed), dims 256 / 8 / 8, M = 1,024 tokens, `decode_k = 6`, TF32 with fp32 weights, one worker; E8 AR only, P3 zero-shot only (no labels bought). The configurations are generated (`scripts/make_fine3d_configs.py`, which refuses E2's file unless its canonical SHA-256 is PREREG_E2's stamped line); a test regenerates them byte for byte and checks that each differs from E2's only in `_comment`, `out`, `prereg_file`, `e8.seed_offset` and, for S, its three model keys. The top-level `seeds` key of E2's file stays as it is: the runner reads the seeds from `experiments.e8`.

Why fresh seeds: E2's own states were read (wp8 4e) and their fine-mesh deficit is why R2a exists; comparing S with them would select on their training noise (PREREG_W9 Sec. 2's argument). E2's seeds 0-2 serve instead as a coarse check of the training path on the box (Sec. 7). The selection on the instances remains: R2a reads the validation split and fine set on which 4e found the deficit, and S's gain should be largest where the deficit is; this favours support, as PREREG_W9 Sec. 6 states for H2.

The factor 2^-10 only sets the level of S's decoded output, by the rule that gave wp9's 1/64: the power of two nearest the median of max|F| / sum|F| on the training family. On 256 instances drawn afresh from the training family's sampler (`scripts/fine3d_scale_factor.py`, generator seed 93000, which no corpus or set has used; assembly only; `records/wp9/fine3d/scale_factor_3d.json`) the median is 0.00113 (10th-90th percentile 0.00068-0.00190, i.e. 0.69-1.95 times 2^-10) and grows with lc (rank correlation 0.62), so at training mesh sizes S asks the network for outputs of about E2's level on the median instance (1.16 times). E2's corpus itself was not read for this, so that its tail -- the 720 instances after the training pool's 1,024, of which R1 (branch cmame-paper, PREREG_HOLDOUT) evaluates 686 -- stays unread. Meshed again at the in-band ends and middle and at the fine lc (16 geometries, the wp8 post-hoc remesh geometries), max|F| at lc 0.0374 is 0.186 times its lc 0.0906 value (median; range 0.168-0.210; lc itself 0.413 times), and sum|F| is unchanged to within 0.07%: the bottleneck's scale shrinks roughly with the face area per node, S's does not. (These medians equal, to the last digit, the ones the box recorded for the same geometries in wp8's post-hoc pass, `records/wp8/posthoc/amp_e2_m1024.json`; 57 of the 64 meshes have the box's node counts, and the other 7 differ from it by 1 to 21 nodes.)

S changes the decode scale and the load inputs together, and R2a tests them together; a SUPPORTED R2a is a statement about S as a whole. The code: the bottleneck takes `decode_scale`, `decode_scale_factor` and `features.load_density` with the FE-JEPA transformer's validation and values (`fejepa.models.features.check_decode_scale`, `decode_scale_value`, `build_features_battery`); the token geometry (seeds, blend weights) does not depend on them; with the defaults the bottleneck is E2's bit for bit (Sec. 9). P3 now takes E8's seed offset: its zero-shot rows read the states E8 trained (`ar_p1024_s3/4/5.pt`) and record those seeds; without an offset it reads seeds 0..n-1 as before, and an offset written into P3's own block is refused.

| Configuration | Seeds | What it is |
|---|---|---|

Both arms are run by `scripts/r3_finetune.py`; the configurations are generated (`scripts/make_fine3d_configs.py`; a test regenerates them byte for byte) and differ only in `_comment`, `out` and `train.loss`. For each seed 0, 1, 2, the Phase-2b AR state (`runs/phase2/e8_states/ar_p1024_s<seed>.pt`, SHA-256 as the Phase-2b report records them: `a7b5c71c...`, `e9c0c400...`, `e0f2210b...`; the report itself `records/wp8/e2/baseline/report_phase2b.json`, SHA-256 `320b6db5...`, as PREREG_E2 Sec. 2 records it), loaded strictly into the report's own model, is fine-tuned on the first 16 instances of the fine few-shot prefix (`instance_00256` to `instance_00271` of `runs/data3d_phase2_fine`: the instances on which P3's labelled fine-tuning at budget 16 trained) with that fine-tuning's schedule (50 epochs, 800 steps; lr 1.5e-3, read from the Phase-2b configuration; AdamW with weight decay 1e-4; gradient clip 1.0; 5% linear warm-up, then cosine) and seed. Both trainers draw each epoch's instance order from the seed, so the two arms visit the 16 instances in the same order, which is the order of P3's labelled fine-tuning (tests check it through the script for both arms; each result records its arm, the seed it trained with and the SHA-256 of the epoch orders that seed determines). By design the arms differ only in the objective: the energy instead of the displacement labels (64 fine solutions, 16 instances x 4 load cases, stored since Phase 2, which the labelled arm reads and the energy arm does not).
- Reference: the labelled arm, run in the same session as the energy arm, after it: on the same commit (the adjudication refuses otherwise), torch, GPU and TF32 setting, host and GPU driver (each seed records its host and driver where it starts, kept across a resumption, on any resumption and where it ends, and the TF32 setting its training runs with; a difference in any of these, or a TF32 setting other than the source report's, is a deviation, Sec. 8). The order cannot favour either arm: nothing a run reads depends on the runs before it (the arms run as separate processes, and each state is checked against its SHA-256 and its recorded predictions before it trains), so the order changes a result only by the round-off in which any two GPU runs differ; interleaving the arms by seed would not save a session cut short either, since R3 needs all six runs. The labelled arm starts only once the energy arm's report is complete (Sec. 8). On CPU the labelled arm reproduces P3's labelled unit at the same budget per instance, bit for bit, with the same trainer configuration field by field (tests, on a miniature of the Phase-2b run); both arms train in fp32 without compilation, as the Phase-2b configuration ran P3's units (the script refuses a source report that says otherwise); both training paths are bitwise unchanged on CPU since the Phase-2b code (the regressions of Sec. 9). The Phase-2b record of the same fine-tuning (P3, `fewshot["16"]["finetune"]`; per seed: displacement 0.0559 / 0.0500 / 0.0533, energy gap 0.0776 / 0.0758 / 0.0573, von Mises 0.1732 / 0.1766 / 0.1468; run 22-28 September 2026 at `prereg-phase2b-r2` with the supervised trainer) is not the reference: it ran on another commit and possibly on another host and GPU driver (on CUDA any two runs differ at round-off), and its energy gap and von Mises error are the values that motivated R3 (Sec. 1), so that a rule held at them would favour support if they lay above their expectation (Sec. 6). The labelled arm is read beside the record (Sec. 7).
- Before each seed trains, in either arm, a content check: the state's predictions on the first 8 fine evaluation instances and the first 8 in-band validation instances must reproduce the report's per-instance displacement errors and energy gaps (P3's zero-shot cell, E8's AR cell) to a relative 1e-3 (wp8's post-hoc pass reproduced them exactly; a wrong state or code path deviates by orders of magnitude more); otherwise, and on any non-finite prediction or deviation, the script stops before training.
- Budget 16 only (the PI's choice of 14 October 2026; the budget of 64 would cost about 13 h per seed and arm). The labelled arm run in the same session is the PI's choice of 15 October 2026.

## 6. Hypotheses and verdicts
Noise guard (the E-series rule, PREREG_E1 r13): for arm values A (new) and B (reference) from three seeds each, rel = mean(A)/mean(B) - 1 and SE_rel = sqrt(s_A^2/3 + s_B^2/3) / mean(B) with sample standard deviations (n - 1); tau = max(10%, 2 x SE_rel). "Lower beyond the guard" means rel < -tau; "worse beyond the guard" means rel > tau. The tau applied is reported with each verdict, as are the per-seed values.
- **R2a (primary).** Reference: `r2a_b_m1024`. **SUPPORTED** iff all of: (i) S's fine displacement error is lower beyond the guard; (ii) S's fine / in-band displacement-error ratio is lower beyond the guard (the growth itself shrinks, not only both levels); (iii) K1, read on E8's in-band cell: neither S's energy gap nor its displacement error there is worse beyond the guard. Otherwise **NOT SUPPORTED**, with the failing conditions named. A non-finite per-seed value of S fails the condition it enters (reported as diverged); a non-finite value of the fresh baseline voids the comparison (R2a not evaluated; its readings and deviations are still reported); a seed mean non-finite in only one of P3's in-band pass and E8's cell is refused instead (Sec. 8). Properties by simulation (`scripts/fine3d_sims.py`, `records/wp9/fine3d/simulations.json`: 200,000 trials per case, three log-normal seed values per arm, the spreads of E2's run -- coefficients of variation 10% and 11% for the fine and the in-band displacement errors, 20% for the in-band energy gap -- for both arms, a seed's values drawn independently): a support without a true effect occurs in 1.4% of trials (1.7% if S's seeds spread 1.5 times as much); a true fine reduction of 20% / 30% / 50% / 70% with the in-band errors unchanged is supported in 38% / 70% / 89% / 90% of trials. The support rate stays near 90% however large the effect, because K1 reads two in-band metrics at the guard's resolution and fires on noise in about one trial in ten (the E-series' own false-kill rate). If S also lowers the in-band errors by 10%, condition (ii) needs more at small effects (21% / 54% / 97% / 98%); if it raises them by 10%, K1 fails more often (37% / 58% / 65% / 65%). These rates rest on three seeds' spreads: with both arms' spreads twice E2's, a true reduction of 30% / 50% / 70% is supported in 25% / 66% / 87% of trials, and with the spreads of E2's M = 512 arm (18%, 48% and 65%) in 11% / 33% / 64%, the rate without an effect staying at or below 1.4%; a positive correlation within a seed between its fine and in-band errors (which three seeds cannot estimate) lowers that rate and raises the others (rho 0.5: 0.8% without an effect; 52% / 85% / 90% / 90%). A NOT SUPPORTED R2a with an observed fine reduction of 30-50% is therefore weak evidence against S.
- **The R2b rule (fixed here, before R2a runs).** R2b -- the paper's transformer with and without S, on three fresh seeds each as here: six runs of about 27 h of training, about a week of one GPU -- is **recommended** iff R2a is SUPPORTED and S's fine displacement error is at most 55% of the fresh baseline's (rel <= -45% in condition (i), compared to within 1e-12 for floating-point rounding). Otherwise it is not recommended on this evidence. A recommendation, not a verdict: the PI decides, and R2b gets its own pre-registration. Why -45% (the same script, `r2b`, `r2b_sensitivity` and `r2b_line`):
  - The decision. R2b is judged by R2a's rule on the seed spreads of the Phase-2b transformer (coefficients of variation 17% for the fine displacement error, 9% and 25% for the in-band displacement error and energy gap, against the bottleneck's 10%, 11% and 20%), which supports a true fine reduction of 30% / 40% / 50% / 70% in 49% / 74% / 87% / 90% of trials. Assume that S's reduction on the transformer is 0.87 times its reduction on the bottleneck (the ratio of the two c* reductions, 68% and 78%, Sec. 1), that the bottleneck's true reduction is uniform on 0-80% (about c*'s 78%) and that S leaves the transformer's in-band errors unchanged; and value a supported R2b at twice its cost, a NOT SUPPORTED one at nothing. Recommending then pays where the support R2b is predicted to reach, given an R2a SUPPORTED whose observed fine rel lies within 2.5 points of the line, passes one half (R2b more likely supported than not), and the line of the largest expected value lies there.
  - The spreads. With the transformer's spreads at their three-seed estimates, that support near -35% / -40% / -45% / -50% is 50% / 60% / 70% / 78%, and the line falls near -35%. But three seeds estimate a spread poorly, and the support depends strongly on it (near -40%: 34% with the transformer's spreads 1.5 times their estimates, 21% with twice). With each of the transformer's spreads drawn in every trial from its uncertainty given three seeds (sigma^2 = 2 s^2 / chi2_2, the posterior under the prior 1/sigma: a spread's median 1.2 times its estimate, more than twice it in 22% of trials; at most 4 times its estimate), the support is 36% / 43% / 50% / 56%, it passes one half at -45%, and -45% is the line of the largest expected value.
  - Sensitivity. The line stays near -45% without the cap or with it at 3 times (-45% and -44%), with the ratio drawn uniform on 0.74-1.0 (-45%), with the bottleneck's spreads drawn too (-46%) and under three other priors (0-60%, half the mass at no effect, decreasing to 0 at 80%: -45% to -46%); it moves to -47% if S changes the transformer's in-band errors by up to 10% either way, and to -53% with a lower ratio (uniform on 0.5-1.0). Milder models of the spreads' uncertainty move it the other way: -42% with the spreads at most 2 times their estimates or one draw for the three metrics, -40% and -38% with a prior worth 2 or 4 more degrees of freedom at the estimates, -35% under the prior 1/sigma^2 (sigma^2 = 2 s^2 / chi2_3). Over these 16 three-seed cases, -45% loses at most 8% of a case's best expected value, -40% up to 25% and -50% up to 16%; in the main case the expected value is flat near its peak (-40% and -50% reach 97% of it). The value assumed moves the line most: with a supported R2b worth three times its cost the best line is -35%, with 1.5 times about -60%.
  - In practice. At -45% (the spreads drawn, the 0-80% prior) R2b is recommended in 39% of trials and supported after 69% of the recommendations, and it would be supported but is not recommended in 15% of trials (at -40%: 45%, 66% and 13%; at -50%: 34%, 71% and 18%). Five seeds per arm (ten runs) raise the supported share of the recommendations (80% at -45%, the spreads drawn) but lower the expected value at every line, with a supported R2b worth 1.5 to 3 times the cost of six runs: R2b's own pre-registration should weigh its number of seeds against its cost. By the simulation of R2a above, a true fine reduction of 50% / 70% on the bottleneck leads to a recommendation in 78% / 90% of trials with the in-band errors unchanged (85% / 98% if S also lowers them by 10%; 56% / 65% if it raises them by 10%), one of 30% in 0.2%.
- **R3-E (primary).** Reference: the labelled arm (`r3_lft_b16`). **SUPPORTED** iff the energy arm's fine relative energy gap is lower than the labelled arm's beyond the guard; otherwise **NOT SUPPORTED** (reported as "worse" if worse beyond the guard; as diverged if a per-seed value of the energy arm is non-finite). A non-finite per-seed value of the labelled arm voids the verdict whose metric it is (NOT EVALUATED, with the reason); the other verdict, the readings and the deviations are still reported.
- **R3-V (primary).** As R3-E, for the fine von Mises relative L2 error.
- Properties of R3-E and R3-V by simulation (the same script; both arms drawn afresh in every trial, with the spreads of the recorded labelled fine-tuning -- coefficients of variation 16% for the energy gap, 10% for the von Mises error -- unless stated): a support without a true effect occurs in 5.4% (R3-E) and 4.3% (R3-V) of trials, in 7.0% and 6.0% if the energy arm spreads 1.5 times as much, and in 13% and 15% if it spreads by 50% (the zero-shot states' fine energy gaps spread by 72%: 0.530 / 0.296 / 0.091; with a skewed spread and three seeds the guard's standard error is itself noisy). A true reduction of 10% / 20% / 30% / 50% is supported in 18% / 45% / 76% / 99.6% of trials (R3-E) and 30% / 76% / 98% / 100% (R3-V). These rates rest on three recorded seeds' spreads, for the reference as for the energy arm: with both arms' spreads twice the record's, a support without an effect occurs in 5.6% of trials (R3-E and R3-V), and a true reduction of 20% / 30% / 50% is supported in 19% / 33% / 72% (R3-E) and 34% / 61% / 97% (R3-V). A NOT SUPPORTED R3-E or R3-V with an observed reduction of 20-30% is therefore weak evidence against the energy objective's advantage. The two arms of a seed share its starting state and instance order, so their values may be correlated, which the guard ignores (it treats the arms as independent): with a correlation of 0.5 between a seed's two values, a support without an effect occurs in 2.4% (R3-E) and 1.2% (R3-V) of trials, and a true reduction of 20% / 30% / 50% is supported in 45% / 80% / 99.8% (R3-E) and 81% / 98.9% / 100% (R3-V). Why the labelled fine-tuning is run again (r1 compared the energy arm with the record): held at the record, the rule's rate without an effect is 0.6% if the record equals its expectation but 6.1% (R3-E) and 18% (R3-V) if it lies 10% above it (the excess, 9% of the record, is about 1.0 and 1.6 times the standard error of the record's mean), and the record is the run whose values motivated R3; the labelled arm also shares the energy arm's commit, host, driver and runtime.
- **What R3-E and R3-V license.** R3-E's metric is the energy-norm error that the energy objective minimises on its training instances, and the von Mises error measures the stress that norm weights, as the displacement error is the labelled objective: they test whether the energy objective's advantage carries to the 256 held-out fine instances at the labelled fine-tuning's schedule (which was not tuned for it), not whether label-free fine-tuning is the more accurate overall. Any write-up reports the fine displacement error against the labelled arm (Sec. 7, with the guard and its direction) beside R3-E and R3-V.
- Three verdicts, no multiplicity correction: they are separate claims, each reported with its own threshold. R3-E and R3-V read the same networks and are not independent evidence. A part whose reports the adjudication refuses (Sec. 8) is NOT EVALUATED, recorded with the reason; the other part is still adjudicated.
- **What a verdict licenses.** SUPPORTED: on these seeds and instances, at this budget, model and training family, the new arm is lower than the reference beyond tau (for R2a, each of its conditions holds). NOT SUPPORTED: no such reduction was shown; it is not evidence that the effect is absent or small. A reduction smaller than tau cannot be shown by design, and one near tau is missed about half the time. No verdict is restated as "no effect"; each is reported with rel, SE_rel, tau and the per-seed values. R2a is a statement about the token bottleneck: whether S does the same for the transformer is R2b's question.

## 7. Secondary readings (reported, no verdict; exploratory)
- **R2a.** Beside each condition: the same guard on per-seed medians over the instances (for the ratio, the per-seed ratio of the medians); rel with a Welch 95% interval; a 95% interval for rel from resampling the instances (2,000 resamples, the same instances for both arms and every seed, the seeds kept). Mechanism, from the post-hoc records: the median c* on both sets (near 1 on the fine set if S removes the deficit), the errors after scaling by c*, and on the remesh geometries, by lc, the predicted energy norm relative to the coarsest mesh (the bottleneck's fell to 0.41-0.58 of its coarsest-mesh value at lc 0.0374 in wp8 4e; S's should not fall) and c_b (the bottleneck's rose from 0.93-0.95 at lc 0.0906 to 1.69-2.37 at 0.0374; S's should stay near 1). If the fresh baseline does not show the deficit -- its fine / in-band ratio of seed means below 1.5 or its median c* on the fine set below 1.10, PREREG_W9's rule-1 lines -- a NOT SUPPORTED R2a is reported as uninformative about S's mechanism; the verdict stands (without the baseline's amplitude record, or when its median c* on the fine set is not finite, the c* part is reported as not evaluated). The fresh baseline beside E2's seeds 0-2 (the fine displacement error, E8's in-band energy gap and displacement error, with the guard): a difference beyond the guard is reported as a flag on the training path and changes nothing.
- **R3.** The fine displacement error, peak von Mises error and critical-region recall, the energy arm against the labelled arm (the displacement error is the labelled arm's own objective and is expected to favour it); every metric of each arm against the zero-shot states (the report's P3 cells); the in-band metrics after fine-tuning, the energy arm against the labelled arm and each arm against E8's AR cell before it (what the fine-mesh training costs in band); the fine / in-band displacement ratio after fine-tuning, per arm; c* and c_b on both sets, per arm; per seed, the paired relative difference between the arms (the reading that uses the shared starting state and instance order); beside R3-E and R3-V, the guard on per-seed medians, the Welch interval and the instance-resampling interval (the same 256 fine instances in both arms). The labelled arm against the Phase-2b record of the same fine-tuning, every metric with the guard and per seed: a difference beyond the guard is reported as a flag on the training path and changes nothing; the energy arm against the record likewise, as a reading.

## 8. Execution mechanics (exact)
- **Stamping**, before any run: `scripts/stamp_prereg_fine3d.py --date "<date>"` fills the four CONFIG_SHA256 lines (each the canonical SHA-256 of its configuration, as `fejepa prereg --stamp` computes it), the status line and the last line, this file's SHA-256, computed over the file with that line reading `PREREG_FINE3D_SHA256 = <record after commit>` (a file cannot contain its own hash). It refuses a file that is not this draft byte for byte, configurations other than the generator's, and a `src` that is not the tree the cross-branch regression ran (Sec. 9). Then commit; run the full suite on that commit; tag it `prereg-fine3d` (an annotated tag); push the branch and the tag; the remote tag is verified before any GPU time. The branch is frozen until the session has returned: before the session and before every step, the box checks that `git describe --always --dirty --tags` -- the string every report records -- prints exactly `prereg-fine3d` and that it is the only tag on the commit.
- **The session** (`RUNBOOK_FINE3D.md`; `scripts/fine3d_session.sh` runs the steps in this order and keeps every attempt's log): `run-config configs/r2a_b_m1024.json`, `run-config configs/r2a_s_m1024.json`, the post-hoc amplitude pass on each, then `scripts/r3_finetune.py configs/r3_eft_b16.json` (the energy arm) and, once the energy arm's report is complete, `scripts/r3_finetune.py configs/r3_lft_b16.json` (the labelled arm: R3 needs both, so a failed energy arm does not spend the labelled arm's time). Every training step is guard-verified against this file: a run-config refuses to start unless its configuration's hash is its own line here; R3's script likewise; the post-hoc passes read those guard-verified reports and the states they record. A step whose output is complete is skipped when the script runs again; an R2a run with states but no report restarts with `--reuse-states` (D9; recorded in its report); an R3 arm resumes an interrupted seed from its epoch checkpoint if this code, configuration and seed wrote it (otherwise that seed trains from the start), does not run again a seed finished by this code and configuration (marking it as taken from disk) and runs again a seed whose result file is unusable. Before every step it runs, the session also checks that the GPU is there, works (an allocation and a synchronisation) and is free (90% of its memory): a step without it is not started (a step must not run on the CPU, nor start on a failing or busy GPU), and the status file says why. A check of a step's output that itself fails (killed, out of memory, an I/O error) moves and runs nothing, and the status file says so before that step. Ctrl-C stops the session once the command it interrupts has ended, with a status line saying so. An epoch checkpoint or a D9 state whose file cannot be read is moved aside and trained again; one that reads but cannot be restored (another model's state, or an error on the device) stops the step and is kept. A remesh geometry that gmsh cannot mesh is recorded and the pass goes on (a second pass would not mesh it either); an error while the states are evaluated stops the pass (the step fails, and the session's next run does it again).
- **Adjudication:** `scripts/adjudicate_fine3d.py`, from the return. It refuses an R2a report that is not a guard-verified run of this file under its own line, whose configuration does not hash to its recorded SHA-256, that ran on another commit than `prereg-fine3d`, differs from E2's configuration beyond the keys of Sec. 2 or is not the stamped arm for its role, ran on another corpus or other seeds, or does not hold one evaluation per seed with a value for each of the set's instances (E8's and P3's cells), or whose P3 in-band pass does not reproduce its E8 AR cell (the seed means of the displacement error and energy gap to a relative 1e-3: the same states on the same split; E2's bottleneck on the box, 3e-5; a seed mean finite in one of the two only fails, and one finite in neither, a diverged seed, is left to Sec. 6); it refuses an R3 report, of either arm, that is not a guard-verified run of this file under its arm's line, whose configuration does not hash to its recorded SHA-256, that is not its arm's configuration (its output directory and objective) or does not have the schedule of the labelled fine-tuning at 16, ran on another commit (the report's or a seed's), has a seed run with another configuration than the report's, ran on another corpus or other seeds, started from another source report or another state, failed its content check, trained with the other arm's objective or trainer, with another seed, on other instances or for another number of epochs or steps (counted from the trainer's history), or lacks a seed's evaluation on either set; it refuses R3's two reports together if they ran on different code or recorded different epoch orders for a seed (each run records the orders its seed determines; tests check that both trainers visit them); and it refuses inputs whose SHA-256 is not the records' (the Phase-2b report, E2's report). A report that is missing from the return or cannot be read leaves its part NOT EVALUATED (R3 needs both arms). A post-hoc amplitude record of another report, or an incomplete one (a seed's in-band or fine pass unfinished or its reproduction unchecked), is not read (its readings are not evaluated); one whose remesh pass is short of its 16 geometries or has a failed one is read without its remesh readings, a deviation; one whose predictions differ from the report's by more than 5% (relative, per instance, an instance non-finite in both not compared; E2's bottleneck on the box: up to 1.1%) is read with a deviation. Restarts, resumed seeds, seeds taken from disk, repeated attempts, steps not started and steps whose last attempt did not exit 0 (from the session's status file; a status file not given or not in the return is itself one), a solve ledger above 0, a different torch, GPU or TF32 setting between the two R2a arms or between the two R3 arms, R3 seeds run on more than one host or GPU driver (a driver that could not be read is not counted as another; records without it beside records with it are a deviation of their own), and R3 seeds trained with another TF32 setting than the source report's are recorded as deviations; a different setting between the labelled arm and the Phase-2b record is recorded beside R3, with the flags of Sec. 7. The verdict file records the adjudicating code -- its `git describe` and its files' SHA-256 -- and whether those files and the pre-registration it read are the tag's; a difference, or a tag that cannot be read there, is a deviation.
