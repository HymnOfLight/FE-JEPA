Supervised training of a finite-element surrogate needs a solve per training example, a
cost that dominates where solves are expensive. For linear elastostatics we train on the assembled
discrete potential energy instead. Minimising it is exactly supervised regression in the
stiffness norm, which measures the strain energy of the stress error, yet no example is
solved. The identity gives the energy three roles: training objective, metric and failure
detector. Without reference solutions, the energy ranks predictions of one load case by
their stiffness-norm error, and its sign flags predictions worse than the zero field. In a pre-registered three-dimensional comparison, a
transformer trained on the energy alone was more accurate on every metric, in each of three
seeds, than the same network trained on the solutions of the same instances. In the same
run, a supervised graph network had the smaller displacement error on most instances and the
larger von Mises error on almost all, a post hoc reading. Every supervised network was worse
than the zero field on some load cases, all flagged by the sign of the energy. In two
dimensions the label-free transformer had a larger displacement error than its supervised
counterpart but smaller errors on every other metric. Trained on the stiffness-norm error
with the same labels, the counterpart more than halved its von Mises error, supporting a
pre-registered hypothesis. No network transferred to a substantially finer mesh without
retraining; a post hoc, label-free rescaling removed most of the label-free network's error
there.
