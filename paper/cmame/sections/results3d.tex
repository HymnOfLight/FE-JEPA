\section{Three dimensions: the three roles}\label{sec:results3d}

In the three-dimensional comparison (\cref{tab:runs}), the label-free transformer, trained
in the amended run, was compared with the supervised transformer, with and without the
energy term, and the graph network, which the initial run had trained on the same corpus,
split and seeds and which were reused after their hashes were checked
(\cref{sec:methods:prereg}); the configuration hash is given in \ref{app:repro}. Of its
pre-registered conditions, the one on the accuracy of the label-free network was met and the
one on transfer failed (\cref{sec:results3d:decisions}); beyond them, the readings of this
section come from the run's records and are not pre-registered criteria. The section takes
the three roles of the energy in turn: the training objective, through the same network
trained with and without labels (\cref{sec:results3d:same}); the evaluation metric, through
the order in which the displacement and the von Mises errors rank the networks
(\cref{sec:results3d:reverse}); and the failure detector, through the predictions worse than
the zero field (\cref{sec:results3d:detection}).

\subsection{Training objective: the same network with and without labels}\label{sec:results3d:same}

\Cref{tab:3d} gives every metric with 1,024 labelled instances. The label-free transformer
was more accurate than the same transformer trained on the solutions of the same instances,
with or without the energy term, on all five metrics, in the seed mean and in each of the
three seeds. Its displacement error, \numThreeFreeDisp{}, was \numThreeFreeDispAdvantage{}
lower than the supervised transformer's, \numThreeLabDisp{}; its relative energy gap,
\numThreeFreeGap{}, was \numThreeAncOverFreeGap{} times lower than that of the supervised
transformer with the energy term, the better of the two supervised variants, a factor of
\numThreeAncOverFreeGapRoot{} in the root-mean-square relative stiffness-norm error; and its
von Mises error (\numThreeFreeVm{} against \numThreeLabVm{} and \numThreeAncVm{}), peak
stress error (\numThreeFreePeak{} against \numThreeLabPeak{} and \numThreeAncPeak{}) and
critical-region recall (\numThreeFreeRecall{} against \numThreeLabRecall{} and
\numThreeAncRecall{}) were better. Compared instance by instance within each seed, the
label-free transformer had the lower relative energy gap on \numPairThreeLabGap{} and
\numPairThreeAncGap{} of the \numThreePairs{} instance-seed pairs against the two supervised
variants, the lower von Mises error on \numPairThreeLabVm{} and \numPairThreeAncVm{}, and
the lower displacement error on \numPairThreeLabDisp{} and \numPairThreeAncDisp{}. The
three-dimensional pre-registration fixed no noise guard; read after the run with the guard
of the two-dimensional comparison, \numThreeGuardWithin{} of the \numThreeGuardTotal{}
differences of seed means lie within it: in relative energy gap and peak stress error
against the supervised transformer, whose seed \numThreeLabWorstSeed{} widens the guard
(below), and in critical-region recall against the variant with the energy term. In two
dimensions, with the same code, the label-free transformer was not the more accurate in
displacement
(\cref{sec:results2d:accuracy}); we have not investigated why the two dimensions differ in
this respect.

The supervised transformer's mean relative energy gap, \numThreeLabGapMs{}, is the mean of
three seeds, \numThreeLabGapSeeds{}. The second is dominated by one validation instance,
No.~\numWorstIndex{}, whose supervised prediction in seed \numThreeLabWorstSeed{} had a
relative energy gap of \numThreeLabWorst{}, averaged over its four load cases, with a
displacement error of \numWorstSeedLabDisp{}, a von Mises error of \numWorstSeedLabVm{} and
a peak stress error of \numWorstSeedLabPeak{}. The same instance had the largest relative
energy gap of the supervised transformer in all three seeds. Supervised regression on the
displacement error does not control the stiffness norm, and with it the stresses: the
median relative energy gap of the supervised transformer, \numThreeLabGapMedian{}, is far
below its mean. The energy term repaired most of this, but not at every budget: with 256
labels it raised the displacement error of the supervised transformer in every seed and,
through one of the three seeds, its mean relative energy gap (\cref{tab:labeleff}),
although it reduced the number of instance-seed predictions worse than the zero field from
\numThreeLabWorseTwoFiveSix{} to \numThreeAncWorseTwoFiveSix{}.

\subsection{Metric: displacement error against von Mises error}\label{sec:results3d:reverse}

\label{fig:reverse}
\end{figure}

The graph network trained with labels had the lowest mean displacement error in
\cref{tab:3d}, \numThreeMgnDisp{}, \numThreeFreeOverMgnDisp{} times lower than the
label-free transformer's, and the label-free transformer was more accurate on every other
metric. The reversal held on most instances individually (\cref{fig:reverse}). Compared
within each seed, the graph network's displacement error was lower on
\numMgnDispLowerMin{} to \numMgnDispLowerMax{} of the instances, but its von Mises error was
higher on \numMgnVmHigher{} of the \numThreePairs{} instance-seed pairs, with a mean
\numThreeMgnOverFreeVm{} times higher, and its relative energy gap was higher on at least
\numMgnGapHigherMin{} of the instances in every seed. On \numMgnLowerDispHigherVm{} pairs
both held at once: a lower displacement error and a higher von Mises error. The peak stress
error, which compares a single value per load case, followed the pattern less closely: the
graph network's was the lower on \numMgnPeakLowerMin{} to \numMgnPeakLowerMax{} of the
instances, although its mean was \numThreeMgnOverFreePeak{} times higher. The relative
energy gap thus ranked the two networks as the von Mises error did, not as the displacement
error did. These per-instance comparisons average $g$ over the load cases and use the
solutions; for the predictions of one load case, the energy ranks them exactly as $g$ does,
without a reference solution (\cref{cor:rank}).

Validation instance No.~\numWorstIndex{} in seed 0 (\cref{fig:field-worst}) shows the
pattern on one instance. Averaged over its four load cases, the label-free transformer had
the largest displacement error of the three networks, \numWorstFreeDisp{}, and the most
accurate stresses, with a von Mises error of \numWorstFreeVm{} and a relative energy gap of
\numWorstFreeGap{}. The supervised transformer and the graph network had displacement
errors of \numWorstLabDisp{} and \numWorstMgnDisp{}, von Mises errors of \numWorstLabVm{}
and \numWorstMgnVm{}, and relative energy gaps of \numWorstLabGap{} and \numWorstMgnGap{}:
both were more accurate in displacement and worse than the zero field in energy. On the
load case of \cref{fig:field-worst}, the \numFigWorstLoad{}, the three displacement fields
have the shape of the reference, the label-free one with too large an amplitude: its
relative displacement error of \numFigWorstFreeDisp{} falls to \numFigWorstFreeDispC{} when
it is rescaled by $\cstar=\numFigWorstFreeC{}$ (\cref{prop:amp}). The stress fields of the
two supervised networks are rough, and more than twice the reference's on
\numFigWorstTwiceLab{} and \numFigWorstTwiceMgn{} of the solid's volume, against
\numFigWorstTwiceFree{} for the label-free transformer. \Cref{fig:field-median} shows a
typical instance, on which the supervised networks' predictions were better than the zero
field and their stress fields still rough. \Cref{rem:rank} permits reversals between the
displacement and the stiffness-norm errors whenever the displacement errors differ by less
than $\kappa^{1/2}$, and \cref{prop:stress} shows why the energy, not the displacement,
follows the stress.

Where the errors lay in the spectrum of $K$ was measured after the run, from the norms of
the errors on every validation instance, load case and seed; \cref{sec:results2d:spectrum}
makes the same measurement in two dimensions, with the full eigendecomposition. The
normalised Rayleigh quotient $\rho$ of \cref{rem:factor} had a median of \numFieldRqFree{}
for the label-free transformer, \numFieldRqLab{} for the supervised transformer and
\numFieldRqMgn{} for the graph network, and it was larger for the supervised transformer
than for the label-free one on \numFieldRqAboveLab{} of the \numFieldLoads{} triples of
seed, instance and load case, and larger for the graph network than for the label-free one
on \numFieldRqAboveMgn{}. The graph network's smaller displacement errors lay far higher in
the spectrum, where by \eqref{eq:factor} they cost more energy and, by \cref{prop:stress},
more stress error.

\subsection{Failure detector: predictions worse than the zero field}\label{sec:results3d:detection}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{generated/fig_energy_gap.pdf}
\caption{Distribution of the relative energy gap, averaged over each instance's four load
cases, over the \numThreePairs{} validation instance-seed pairs, 1,024 labelled instances,
in three dimensions (left) and in the two-dimensional comparison (right). Markers show the
medians; predictions to the right of the dashed line are further from the solution, in the
stiffness norm, than the zero field, on average over their load cases.}
\label{fig:energygap}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:energygap} shows the distribution of the relative energy gap. In three
dimensions the label-free transformer's median, \numThreeFreeGapMedian{}, was
\numThreeMedianRatioMin{} to \numThreeMedianRatioMax{} times lower than the supervised
networks' medians, and none of its \numThreePairs{} instance-seed predictions had a mean
relative energy gap above one. The supervised transformer had \numThreeLabWorse{} such
predictions, the supervised transformer with the energy term \numThreeAncWorse{} and the
graph network \numThreeMgnWorse{}. These counts use the solutions. Load case by load case,
measured after the run with the test of \cref{cor:zero}, which does not,
\numFieldLoadWorseLab{} of the supervised transformer's \numFieldLoads{} predictions and
\numFieldLoadWorseMgn{} of the graph network's were worse than the zero field, against
\numThreeFreeLoadWorse{} of the label-free transformer's; the per-load-case energies of the
supervised transformer with the energy term were not exported. After the rescaling by
$\cstar$ none of them was worse than the zero field, and the rescaling never increased a
relative energy gap.

These load cases are found without a reference solution, since by \cref{cor:zero} each is
one with $\Pi_h(u)>0$, and rescaling by $\cstar$ makes each no worse than the zero field
(\cref{prop:amp}); the check applies to any network, however it was trained. It catches
gross failures only: the supervised transformer's median prediction, with a
relative energy gap of \numThreeLabGapMedian{}, passed it.

\subsection{Pre-registered decisions}\label{sec:results3d:decisions}

The gate of the three-dimensional comparison combined three conditions as (a) and ((b) or
(c)) (\cref{tab:criteria}): (a) a sanity check on the supervised side, (b) the label-free
transformer's displacement error within 10\% of the supervised transformer's with 1,024
labels and an advantage in relative energy gap of at least 40\% at every budget, and (c)
transfer to the finer mesh. Condition (b), the pre-registered test of the label-free
network's accuracy, was met with a wide margin: the label-free displacement error was
\numGTwoParity{} relative to the supervised one, and the smallest advantage in relative
energy gap was \numGTwoAdvMin{}; the kill conditions on that accuracy, KP1 and KP2, were not
triggered. Condition (c) failed, and kill condition KP4 withdrew the transfer claim
(\cref{sec:transfer}). Condition (a) was met from 64 labels on, but with 16 labels the
supervised transformer with the energy term was only \numGTwoSanitySixteen{} times more
accurate in displacement than the zero field, against the \numGTwoSanityX{} required. The
amendment, written after this result of the initial run was known, assessed (a) only from
\numGTwoFloor{} labels: under it the gate read GO, and under the registered form, which
assesses every budget, it read NO-GO (\ref{app:prereg}). The claims of this section rest on
condition (b) and on the measurements, not on the gate's verdict.
